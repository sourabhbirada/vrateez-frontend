Half of most Indian teams now work from home at least part of the week, and Diwali gifting has not caught up. The box still arrives at reception, sits there for ten days, and reaches the remote half of the team in late November, if at all.

Getting a gift to two hundred doorsteps on the same day is a logistics job, not a shopping job. This checklist is the version that works, in the order you need to do it. Diwali this year is Sunday, 8 November, so the dates assume boxes should land by Dhanteras, Friday the 6th.

## 1. Decide the budget per head, then stop moving it

Most companies land between ₹300 and ₹700 per person for hampers, and ₹1,000 to ₹2,500 for senior staff or long-service gifts. Fix the number before you look at catalogues, because catalogues are built to move it upward.

Add 10 percent for shipping to homes and 5 percent for spare boxes. The spares are not optional; you will need them for new joiners, the person who moved house, and the courier that loses one.

## 2. Collect addresses without creating a privacy problem

A shared spreadsheet where everyone types their own address, visible to everyone else, is the most common mistake and the easiest to avoid.

Use a simple form with five fields: name, phone, full address, pincode, delivery note (gate, floor, landmark). Say in the form exactly what the data is for, who will see it (HR and the courier), and when it will be deleted (one week after delivery). Make it optional, with office pickup as the alternative. Close the form two weeks before dispatch and chase the stragglers personally; a reminder email gets a 60 percent response, a WhatsApp message gets the rest.

## 3. Choose a box that survives a courier

Hampers are designed to look good on a table, not inside a van for four days. Before you commit:

- Ask for a sample and drop it from waist height. Jars crack, biscuits crumble, chocolate melts in transit until late November in most of India.
- Prefer sealed, shelf-stable food: roasted makhana, nut bars, cookies in a tin, dry fruit in pouches. Avoid fresh mithai for anything shipped more than one day.
- Check the best-before date. Festive stock is often produced months ahead. Ask for a production date within the last four weeks.
- Read the first three ingredients on each food item. Refined flour, sugar and palm oil at the top of the list is a cheap box in expensive packaging, and your team will notice.

## 4. One supplier, one invoice, one dispatch date

Splitting the order across suppliers to save ₹40 a box costs you a week of coordination. Choose one supplier who can do three things: ship to individual addresses pan-India, print a card with your message and logo, and give you tracking numbers in a spreadsheet. Agree the dispatch date in writing.

## 5. Work backwards from Dhanteras

| Target | Date for Diwali 2026 | What must be done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boxes at doorsteps | Friday, 6 November | Nothing left to do except reply to thank-you messages |
| Dispatch | Friday, 30 October to Monday, 2 November | Supplier ships, tracking sheet shared with HR |
| Address sheet to supplier | Monday, 26 October | Form closed, addresses checked for missing pincodes |
| Order confirmed and paid | Friday, 16 October | Sample approved, card artwork signed off |
| Budget and headcount fixed | Friday, 2 October | Tiers decided, form sent to employees |

Missed those dates this year? For metro cities, an order placed by Tuesday 3 November can still land by the 7th with a good courier. For smaller towns, shift the message to Bhai Dooj on the 10th and say so on the card; a gift that arrives on a named day is better than one that arrives late with no explanation.

## 6. Write a card that sounds like a person

The printed company line is fine. The problem is when it is the only line. Three approaches that scale:

- For teams under 50, the manager hand-signs every card with one specific line.
- For teams under 300, print the company message and have each team lead add a sentence per direct report; the supplier can print variable text if you give them a sheet.
- For larger teams, print one strong message and send a personal WhatsApp or Slack note from the manager on the day the box lands.

Avoid the phrase "valued member of the family" and anything about "synergy". Say thank you for one real thing the team did this year.

## 7. Tell people the box is coming

A gift that arrives unannounced at a home address, when the employee is at a co-working space or travelling, goes back to the courier. The day boxes dispatch, send one message: what is coming, roughly when, which courier, and whom to call if nobody is home. Share tracking numbers individually if the supplier gives them to you; a sheet with 200 tracking IDs posted in a channel is not the same thing.

## 8. Handle the exceptions before they become complaints

Three things will go wrong with any shipment of 100 or more boxes.

- **Damaged in transit.** Replace first, ask questions later. Keep your spares for this.
- **Wrong address.** Usually a missing flat number. Call the employee, correct it with the courier the same day.
- **Someone was left out.** New joiners, contractors, people on leave. Have a list of who is in and who is not, and a plan for both. A ₹500 gift sent to a contractor who was excluded last year does more for retention than a ₹2,000 gift to someone who expected it.

Give employees one WhatsApp number for problems and answer it within the hour that week.

## 9. Keep the finance team happy

Ask the supplier for a single GST invoice with the company's GSTIN and a line-item description that matches your expense policy. In India, employer gifts above ₹5,000 per employee in a financial year are generally treated as a taxable perquisite; most festive hampers sit well below that, but check with finance if you are combining Diwali gifts with other rewards in the same year.

## 10. Measure whether it worked

You will know by the second week of November. Count the thank-you messages, look at the photos people post, and ask three employees in different cities one question: did it arrive on time and was it worth opening? Write the answers down with the supplier's name and the cost per box. Next September, when the same job comes around, that note is the whole plan.

## The one-page version

Fix the budget, collect addresses properly, pick a box that ships well, use one supplier, work back from Dhanteras, write a human card, tell people it is coming, keep spares, get one invoice, and ask three people if it was worth it. Ten steps, one afternoon of planning in September, and the remote half of the team stops being an afterthought every November.
